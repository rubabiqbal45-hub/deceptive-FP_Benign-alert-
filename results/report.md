## 3. Risk-controlled suppression

Two guarantees are evaluated because they answer different questions:

* **Mode A** bounds the FN rate *inside the suppressed set* (alpha = 2.0%, effective 1.50%).
* **Mode B** (primary) bounds the share of **all** attacks that are suppressed (beta = 2.0%, effective 1.50%).

Mode A is the textbook selective-classification guarantee, but with a low alert base rate it can discard a large share of real attacks while still obeying its bound. Mode B is the guarantee an SOC actually needs.

| Mode | Bound | Alert suppression | FN within suppressed | Attacks missed | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: alpha among suppressed | 0.0148 | 97.98% | 0.0086 | 29.93% | 70.07% |
| B: beta of all attacks | 0.0172 | 52.17% | 0.0004 | 0.65% | 99.35% |

Escalation band: the top 30% of kept alerts by score.

Action mix on the test stream: **suppress** 8,538, **rank** 5,448, **escalate** 2,381.

## Limitations

1. The alert stream is **synthetic**. It validates the pipeline and the evaluation methodology, not real-world efficacy. External validity must come from the lab capture (`lab/`) or public datasets (BOTS, Mordor, EVTX-ATTACK-SAMPLES).
2. Benign activity is generated from playbooks and is therefore cleaner than a real enterprise. Adversarial and drift injection only partly compensate.
4. Queue parameters (arrival intensity, handling time) are assumed, not measured. A live SOC pilot is required before any operational claim.
5. Label noise and alert duplication are not modelled explicitly.
